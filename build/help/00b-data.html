The project data

Everything the project needs is in /data/examples/per3, read-only, next to the guide. These are small pieces cut out of very large public data sets – the parts around PER3 – so that they run in your browser. README.md in that folder says the same as this page.

reference/ – the human genome around PER3

FileWhat it is
GRCh38_chr1_8Mb.fa.gz (+ .fai, .gzi)The first 8,100,000 bases of chromosome 1 of the reference genome GRCh38 (NCBI NC_000001.11), compressed with bgzip and indexed. The sequence is called chr1 and its positions are those of the whole chromosome, so chr1:7829834-7830161 means the same here as anywhere else.
genes_GRCh38.gff3The seven protein-coding genes of chr1:7.4–8.1 Mb – CAMTA1, VAMP3, PER3, UTS2, TNFRSF9, PARK7, ERRFI1 – with the exons and coding sequence of their MANE Select transcript (Ensembl).
PER3_exons.bedThe 22 exons of PER3's MANE Select transcript (ENST00000377532), numbered.
dbsnp157_per3_500kb.vcf.gz (+ .tbi)The 209,029 variants of dbSNP build 157 between chr1:7,580,000 and 8,080,000 – their positions, alleles and rsIDs – to give the pangenome's variants their names (stage 7).

hprc/ – 231 long-read genomes

FileWhat it is
hprc_per3_vntr.vcf.gz (+ .tbi)The variants of the 462 haplotypes of 231 people at chr1:7,829,001–7,831,000 – the repeat and 2 kb around it – from the pangenome of the Human Pangenome Reference Consortium (release 2, Minigraph-Cactus graph v2.1, GRCh38-based VCF before decomposition: one record per "bubble" of the graph, with the whole sequence of each allele). Phased genotypes: 0|1. Used in stages 3–5.
hprc_per3_500kb.vcf.gz (+ .tbi)The same 231 people, chr1:7,580,000–8,080,000, from the decomposed VCF of the same graph (wave: each record a SNP, an indel or a larger variant). 7,441 records. Used in stage 7.
hprc_samples.tsvThe 231 people: population (the 1000 Genomes codes, such as GBR or YRI), its name (as the 1000 Genomes Project writes it), the continental group (AFR, AMR, EAS, EUR, SAS), sex, and whether the person is also in the 1000 Genomes high-coverage data. HG002 (Ashkenazi Jewish) and HG005 (Han Chinese) come from Genome in a Bottle.

The haploid reference genome of the pangenome, CHM13, was left out of these files; so were the INFO fields AF, NS and AT and the graph's names for the variants.

1000g/ – 3,202 short-read genomes

FileWhat it is
1kgp_per3_gene.vcf.gz (+ .tbi)The phased SNVs, indels and structural variants of the 3,202 people of the high-coverage 1000 Genomes Project across PER3, chr1:7,780,000–7,850,000 (New York Genome Center, phased panel of 2022). 1,775 variants. Used in stages 8 and 9.
1kgp_samples.tsvTheir population, continental group and sex, their parents if they are in the data, and whether they are one of the 2,504 unrelated people of the original project (unrelated_2504).

gwas/ – genome-wide association studies of sleep

The results of GWAS of UK Biobank (people of European ancestry) for every variant between chr1:7,600,000 and 8,200,000 – in GRCh37 positions, as the studies published them. Downloaded from the GWAS Catalog. Each row is a variant; the effect belongs to ALLELE1 (or effect_allele).

gtex/ – gene expression

GTEx_v8_PER3_rs228701.tsvFor 49 tissues of GTEx v8 – 13 of them parts of the brain – the effect of the SNP rs228701 on the amount of PER3 RNA (the normalised effect size, NES, of its alternative allele G, and its p-value) and how many donors have each genotype. From the GTEx Portal's API (stage 9; you can also ask the API yourself).

primates/ – PER3 in other species

FileWhat it is
PER3_repeat_exon_DNA.faThe DNA of the repeat's exon and about 150 bases around it, in the direction of the gene, from 35 genomes of 28 primate species: the reference assemblies of NCBI RefSeq, and for the apes both haplotypes of their complete (T2T) genomes. Found with BLAST of the human exon.
PER3_proteins.faThe PER3 protein (its longest RefSeq isoform) of 53 species: primates and, for comparison, colugo (only a fragment), mouse, rat, dog, cattle, opossum and chicken.
species.tsvThe species, their common names and groups (great apes, gibbons, Old World monkeys, New World monkeys, tarsiers, lemurs and lorises, other mammals).

  • GWAS: Jones et al. 2019 (Nature Communications 10: 343 and 10: 1585), Dashti et al. 2019 (10: 1100) and 2021 (12: 900), Lane et al. 2019 (Nature Genetics 51: 387–393); the GWAS Catalog: Cerezo et al. 2025, Nucleic Acids Research 53: D998–D1005. This research used data of UK Biobank, through those studies.
  • GTEx: GTEx Consortium 2020, Science 369: 1318–1330.
  • Ape genomes: Yoo et al. 2025, Nature 641: 401–418. Other species: NCBI RefSeq (O'Leary et al. 2016, Nucleic Acids Research 44: D733–D745).
  • GRCh38: Genome Reference Consortium; dbSNP: Sherry et al. 2001, Nucleic Acids Research 29: 308–311; Ensembl: Dyer et al. 2025, Nucleic Acids Research 53: D948–D957.
